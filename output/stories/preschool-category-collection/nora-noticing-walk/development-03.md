# Development: Nora's Noticing Walk, revision 3

Source: Story 4 in `output/pdf/preschool-story-sheets.pdf`.

## Stage A - Behavior brief

```yaml
lesson: Patient noticing can turn disappointment into discovery and kindness.
desired_behavior: Nora pauses, observes wildlife without chasing or touching, and makes room for a younger child to participate.
real_world_conflict: Nora expects a blue butterfly on a family walk; it does not appear, and Jo startles a bird while trying to help.
undesired_interpretation: A child is owed a wildlife encounter; touching or collecting wildlife is helpful; disappointment is bad; Nora must include Jo because an adult orders her to.
```

## Stage B - Story DNA

```yaml
character: Nora, an energetic four-year-old observer; Mama; younger cousin Jo
purpose: Fill four notebook boxes with things noticed on a family nature walk.
personality: Focused, imaginative, quick to react, and capable of warm repair.
conflict: Nora fixates on a blue butterfly and blames Jo when his shout startles a bird.
emotional_arc: eager -> puzzled -> irritated -> understood -> curious -> absorbed -> empathetic -> delighted -> content
parent_role: Mama validates both children's hopes and poses a safety-framed question; she does not supply the central choice.
child_decision: Nora invents a gentle noticing rule, remembers Jo's hurt, and gives him a real turn.
repeated_phrase: Eyes look, ears hear, gentle hands stay near.
resolution: Their shared noticing fills all four boxes; Nora later enjoys the butterfly without chasing it.
primary_dramatic_question: Can Nora fill her noticing boxes and make the walk good again when the butterfly she expects does not appear?
climax_spread: 11
secondary_behaviors:
  - Wildlife boundaries create the method for finding surprises.
  - Naming weather signs follows from the shared noticing game and fills the established final box.
```

### Necessity tests

1. Because Nora wants a concrete walk mission, she creates four boxes, which establishes the goal and open space for surprise.
2. Because one box is reserved for a butterfly, Nora searches the colored garden, which makes its absence matter.
3. Because Jo wants to help with that search, he calls out and startles a bird, which creates both disappointment and relationship pressure.
4. Because Nora's blame hurts Jo, Mama names both hopes, which helps Nora reconsider without shame.
5. Because Nora needs a different way to continue, she invents a gentle noticing rule as the path curves to the creek, which changes the plan.
6. Because the new plan slows her attention, Nora sees a snail's trail, which fills the first box.
7. Because a nearby sound directs the next look, Nora and Jo find a pill bug and step back, which practices the rule and fills box two.
8. Because Jo asks for a turn and Nora remembers his hurt, she makes room for him, which repairs the relationship and reveals ants.
9. Because Jo finds the ants, Nora records them and recognizes him as a partner, which fills box three and strengthens their shared goal.
10. Because one box remains, Nora explicitly asks Jo what else they can notice, which turns weather signs into the final search.
11. Because Jo reads those signs, he predicts rain and a drop fills box four, which answers the primary question.
12. Because the family returns from the rain with a changed way of looking, Nora sees the later butterfly without chasing it, which resolves her emotion and closes the story.

## Stage D - Critique and revision

- Lesson shown, not lectured: pass. The rule changes what the children do and discover.
- Concrete purpose and recognizable conflict: pass. Four boxes and an expected butterfly are immediately visible and countable.
- Emotional dip without guilt: pass. Mama validates both hopes; Jo's hurt prompts empathy but no manipulative sadness.
- Child agency and causal payoff: pass. Nora invents the method, repairs with Jo, and invites the final shared search.
- One dominant dramatic question: pass. All discoveries fill the same four-box goal; the butterfly coda is reaction and closure.
- Distinct, necessary spreads: pass after revision. Spread 5 now explicitly carries the path from garden to creek; spread 7 has an audible trigger from stone to log; spreads 10-11 make Nora's invitation and Jo's weather inference cause the final observation.
- Safe, realistic nature behavior: pass. The children watch, listen, step back, and record; they never collect or touch wildlife.
- Repetition and read-aloud pleasure: pass. The refrain occurs twice, supported by color, counting, sound words, and a gentle final echo.
- Child, parent, and transfer tests: pass. The four-box question sustains curiosity; the repair is warm rather than corrective; the short phrase transfers to real walks.

## Stage F - Production handoff

### Story spec

- Age: 4
- Mode: parent read-aloud
- Length: 12 spreads
- Primary behavior: patient noticing after disappointment
- Supporting behavior: kindness through making room for a younger child's contribution

### Character requirements

- Nora must remain recognizable through a clear progression: eager focus, sharp disappointment, physical softening, absorbed attention, active inclusion, and quiet contentment.
- Jo is younger and enthusiastic, not careless or naughty. His droop is brief; his confidence returns through real participation.
- Mama is calm and attentive. She validates and asks one useful question but never performs Nora's repair or solves the notebook goal.

### Illustration requirements

- Maintain a continuous route from home to garden path to creek path and back home; spread 5 must visibly join garden and creek.
- The notebook always contains exactly four boxes. Marks accumulate as: swirl, dotty circle, three dashes, raindrop.
- Keep the same blue butterfly absent until spread 12. Do not hide it as an earlier visual joke.
- Depict the sparrow at a safe distance, realistic snail movement, pill-bug curling, ants carrying crumbs, and credible pre-rain signs.
- Observation must never look like collection or handling. Keep hands near bodies and children outside wildlife movement paths.
- Preserve clear space for the refrain and interactive question without covering animals, faces, notebook marks, or weather clues.
